Special Containment Procedures

SCP-7432 (designated Connor Troy) is to be housed in a reinforced personnel suite within the Aurora Annex of Site-Epsilon, furnished to resemble an ordinary residential apartment. SCP-7432 retains standard personnel privileges, including limited internet access, recreational materials, and supervised outdoor time, contingent on continued cooperation and emotional stability, as assessed daily by the assigned on-site counselor.

At all times, SCP-7432 is to be accompanied by no fewer than two members of Task Force Halcyon, both trained in de-escalation procedures and briefed on the subject's known emotional triggers. Physical restraint of SCP-7432 is not authorized under any circumstance short of an active Overflow event (see Description), as such measures have been demonstrated to accelerate, rather than prevent, escalation.

Structures within a 15-meter radius of SCP-7432's containment suite are to be reinforced to withstand sustained kinetic displacement of up to 6 tonnes. In the event SCP-7432 enters a partial or full Overflow state, all non-essential personnel are to evacuate the annex, and SCP-7432's designated handler, currently Research Head Marcus Whitfield, is to attempt vocal de-escalation before any further intervention is considered. Deployment of SCP-7432 for sanctioned field operations requires signed authorization from the Site-Epsilon Director and no fewer than two members of the Overseer Council.

Description

SCP-7432 is a human male, appearing to be in his early twenties, who exhibits consistent and reproducible control over an anomalous kinetic force hereafter referred to as SCP-7432-1 ("the variable"). SCP-7432-1 manifests as a faintly luminescent, pale green emission surrounding SCP-7432's body during periods of concentration or emotional distress, and allows SCP-7432 to exert force on physical objects at a distance, without physical contact, up to an approximate radius of 40 meters under calm conditions.

Observed applications of SCP-7432-1 include telekinetic displacement of objects and individuals, localized barrier generation sufficient to deflect small-arms fire, and a marked, temporary increase in SCP-7432's own physical strength, reaction speed, and structural durability when the variable is drawn inward rather than projected outward3. SCP-7432 has described this inward state, internally referred to by Task Force Halcyon as "Cordial Form," as feeling "like turning the volume down on everything so I can actually hear myself think."

Critically, the intensity of SCP-7432-1 correlates inversely with SCP-7432's ability to regulate his own emotional state. Under calm conditions, output remains within the containment thresholds described above. However, prolonged emotional suppression, frustration, or distress has been observed to produce a cascading amplification effect, designated an Overflow event, during which SCP-7432's control over SCP-7432-1 degrades substantially, output radius and force expand by a factor of 4 to 30, and SCP-7432 has, on three recorded occasions, displayed no memory of the event afterward. It is for this reason, and SCP-7432's demonstrated willingness to cooperate with Foundation personnel, that SCP-7432 has been classified Thaumiel: Overflow-state output has been successfully redirected on two occasions to neutralize hostile anomalies that had resisted conventional containment (see Addendum 7432.2).

SCP-7432 was recovered following an incident at ██████████ Secondary School, in which a reported "localized earthquake" confined to a single classroom left no structural damage outside a 12-meter radius, but displaced seventeen desks, four support beams, and one exterior wall without any conventional mechanism. SCP-7432 was located three streets away, uninjured and unaware that anything anomalous had occurred.

Behaviorally, SCP-7432 presents as even-tempered and largely unbothered by his containment circumstances, cooperating with staff requests without complaint or apparent resentment. Assigned personnel describe his demeanor as laid-back to the point of indifference on most matters, though he has been observed to fixate, with disproportionate curiosity, on specific details that other staff would regard as trivial — the stitching pattern on a jacket, the exact shade of a wall, why a particular hallway light flickers on a two-second delay. SCP-7432 does not display marked enthusiasm even toward events staff would expect to please him, but is reliably willing to initiate and sustain conversation with on-site personnel, and has developed informal rapport with several members of Task Force Halcyon.

Addendum 7432.1 — Interview Log

Interviewed: SCP-7432
Interviewer: Dr. Marcus Whitfield

Dr. Whitfield: Walk me through what it feels like, right before.

SCP-7432: It's not really a feeling, it's more like... a pressure. Like when you're holding a door shut against wind and your arms are shaking and you know eventually you're just going to get tired. I don't get angry, exactly. I just get tired of holding the door.

Dr. Whitfield: And afterward?

SCP-7432: Afterward there's no door. I just find out later what happened to the room.
Addendum 7432.2 — Cordial Protocol, Field Deployment Log

Following six months of cooperative behavioral conditioning, SCP-7432 was authorized for a single supervised field deployment against SCP-████, an unrelated Keter-class entity that had resisted three prior conventional containment attempts. Under direct handler supervision, SCP-7432 was deliberately, gradually destabilized under controlled conditions to induce a directed Overflow state, oriented and released toward the target entity. SCP-████ was successfully immobilized for 74 minutes, sufficient time for Mobile Task Force Sigma-9 to complete re-containment. SCP-7432 required 11 hours of subsequent rest and reported only "a bad headache and missing lunch." The Cordial Protocol has since been added to Site-Epsilon's standing deployment options, pending Overseer Council review before each use.

Addendum 7432.3 — Personnel Note (Dr. Whitfield)

He does better with a schedule and someone checking in on the small stuff — whether he ate, whether he slept, whether the guy down the hall said something stupid again. It's not complicated. Give him a door he doesn't have to hold shut by himself, and there's nothing here that needs containing. Take that away, and we start writing incident reports.
